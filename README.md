# Local Systems Theory

Local Systems Theory (LST) is a framework and educational program for designing systems that remain coherent, truthful, and resilient under compression.

Instead of optimizing for scale, LST emphasizes:

- **Compression over expansion**: reducing a system to expose hidden assumptions and dependencies.
- **Friction as information**: treating conflict, resistance, and process tension as diagnostic signals.
- **Proximity to consequences**: keeping decision-making close to the people affected.
- **Governance under constraint**: building processes that survive disagreement and failure without relying on abstraction or distance.

## Program Snapshot

The LST program is structured as a multi-module, cohort-based learning experience centered on building real systems for small teams (typically 5–15 people), stress-testing them, and documenting failure modes.

Key learning themes include:

1. Compression testing
2. Governance in live conflict
3. Material and operational resistance
4. Temporal compression
5. Anti-viral/anti-fragile network design

## Who It Is For

This material is geared toward practitioners who design and run systems in the real world, including:

- founders and operators,
- engineering and product leaders,
- organizational designers,
- educators and researchers in systems thinking.

## Philosophy in One Line

> If a system cannot remain truthful when made smaller, it was never structurally coherent.
